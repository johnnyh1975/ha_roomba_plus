# Roomba+ 4.5.0

> **Betas: [4.5.0b1](v4.5.0b1.md), [4.5.0b2](v4.5.0b2.md), [4.5.0b3](v4.5.0b3.md), [4.5.0b4](v4.5.0b4.md), [4.5.0b5](v4.5.0b5.md), [4.5.0b6](v4.5.0b6.md).** This file
> collects everything 4.5.0 brings; the release adds the headline.

## Rooms a mission was only sent to say so

Without room tracking and before the cloud has answered, a mission's
`last_cleaned_rooms` holds the rooms it was **sent** to. A room the
robot never reached looked cleaned (#196, side note).

- The list stays, because nothing better is known yet, but the record
  now says what it is: `rooms_source: requested`.
- The vacuum shows it as `last_cleaned_rooms_source`, the
  *Last mission* sensor as `cleaned_rooms_source`. Other values:
  `cloud` (the cloud's room events decide), `tracked` and
  `awaiting_cloud` (room tracking's guess), none (rooms room tracking
  observed, and imported records).
- When the cloud's room events arrive, they replace the list and the
  mark becomes `cloud`, as before.
- Unchanged: the per-room history counts the requested rooms as it did.
  Without tracking and without a cloud answer they are all there is.

## Sending the robot home leaves the room

@Thonno's suggestion: a room abandoned by sending the robot home
(`hmUsrDock`) is not cleaned. The cloud's own room event for it says
"interrupted" and counts nowhere; room tracking counted it as cleaned.
It is now recorded like a skip: in `skipped_rooms`, and left out of
`last_cleaned_rooms`. Only on a mission with planned rooms; a
whole-home run has no room to leave. The last room's skip, which
pauses and docks, is recorded once.

## Mission names by map and by type

A mission's room names were looked up by number alone, across every
map, rooms before zones. Two cases went wrong:

- a **zone** with the same number as a room was recorded under the
  room's name;
- a mission on a **second map** was named from the active map when
  both maps have a room with that number.

The mission's names, the planned room order and the plan room tracking
works through now come from one lookup (`CommandRegionNames`): the
command's own map first, then the active map, then the others, and
within each a zone among zones and a room among rooms.

## Reconfigure to the local connection removes the Prime-only entities

4.4.4 lets *Reconfigure* move a robot that ran cloud-only to the local
connection (Combo j7+/j9+). Its Prime-only entities stayed behind as
unavailable. They are now removed at the switch, before the reload: the
entities whose unique id carries `_prime_`, which the local path never
creates. Entities both paths create keep their unique id and stay the
same entity, with name, area and history.

## 900-series rooms, checked against a real flat

The maintainer's 980, with its rooms marked cell by cell by hand and
every stored mission held against them. Six missions were clean; the
areas cut from them got five rooms right to 85% and more. What the robot
showed was worse, for four reasons, all fixed:

- **A robot carried back is not drift.** Stuck, carried to the dock, no
  pickup counted: the dock measured it 6.2 m from where it thought it
  was, and that was spread over the path after the stuck as drift (the
  robot's real drift: 0.2 and 0.5 m). A dock correction over 1.5 m now
  counts as carried. That stretch is drawn and kept in the trajectory,
  and left out of the grid, the areas and the doors, and out of the
  drift history.
- **Three missions since the grid was empty.** The wait before the first
  areas counted the robot's completed missions over its life. After the
  grid was emptied the areas came from one mission -- the carried one.
  `GridStore.missions_learned` counts what the grid learned from.
- **Two named rooms stay two.** The smeared grid let the hall's area
  take in a named bedroom, and the bedroom's name was gone. An area that
  takes in most of two differently named rooms is now divided between
  them again.
- **A door is where the robot went through.** Rooms either side of a
  wall touch on the grid: four of ten doors on this flat were walls. A
  door now counts once a mission went from deep in one area to deep in
  the other through it. Every real door was crossed; no wall was. Until
  any mission has been checked, every door counts, as before. A door
  that no longer lies where its two areas meet is removed (one into a
  wall stayed for weeks).

And a mission over the whole floor is recorded with the named rooms its
position went into, not with every named room: one run was credited
with a bedroom it spent 1% of its time in, and not with the room it
spent 86% in (that one had no name yet).

Not fixed: the dressing room shares an area with part of the main
bedroom, whose other part is an area of its own. That is how the floor
divides, not a wrong input.

## 900-series map: straight walls, doors and furniture

The areas a 900-series robot's floor is divided into were drawn as the
floor it drove: ragged edges, every bed a hole. Checked against the
maintainer's flat, the walls behind them are simple, and the map now
draws them:

- **Straight walls.** Each room is a rectangle at the outermost floor the
  robot drove; two rooms whose sides lie within a wall's thickness (plus
  a margin) share one wall; a ragged boundary between two areas becomes
  one straight line. Areas with one name are one room.
- **Furniture.** Floor inside a room that nothing drove is drawn as
  furniture (the beds).
- **Doors** are gaps where the robot went through, across the direction
  it crossed in. An opening clearly wider than a door is drawn as an open
  passage.
- **Only where the walls are straight.** Drawn when a large share of the
  robot's travel runs along two axes (the maintainer's 980: 42% within 2
  degrees); otherwise the areas are drawn as before.
- **New settings** (900-series only): *Wall thickness* (default 15 cm)
  and *Door width* (default 80 cm).
- Not over walls or doors you drew yourself.

Diagnostics: `rooms.layout` says whether the layout is drawn, and why
not (`axis_share` against `needs_axis_share`), with counts only.

### Smart Map and Prime: walls and doors on the rooms map

The rooms come from iRobot's own map and are straight already. What the
map does not draw on a Classic robot are walls and doors:

- **Doors where two rooms meet.** iRobot puts its room dividers across
  the openings between rooms, so where two rooms' polygons touch is an
  opening: a door (thin green line) or, clearly wider than the set door
  width, an open passage.
- **Walls everywhere else**, as thick as set; two rooms' walls a wall's
  thickness apart are one wall, halfway between.
- **Prime** maps that carry iRobot's own floor plan keep its walls; the
  doors are marked on top. Without one, the walls come from the rooms as
  on Classic.
- **The door width** also bounds the door gaps a Smart Map robot's path is
  lined up with its rooms by (20 cm narrower to 40 cm wider; 80 cm gives
  the window it always had).
- *Wall thickness* and *Door width* are offered for these robots too.

Not checked against a real multi-room map yet: the only Classic Smart Map
capture we have has one room. Diagnostics now count the map's own door
and threshold layers (`map_doors`, `map_thresholds`) and what the rooms
map draws (`rooms_map_walls`), all counts.

Tried and left out: filling the furniture in before the floor is
divided. On the maintainer's flat it split a bedroom in two; on the
second 980's home the rule found no furniture to fill.

## Japanese, Chinese and Korean room names on the maps

On the maps these names were boxes (@kyoheimukaida, Combo j9+): the
labels' font, DejaVu Sans, has no CJK characters. A label now falls back
to the first font that has all its characters -- the fonts in
`roomba_plus/fonts` in the configuration folder, which an update keeps,
then fonts the system has. Roomba+ does not bundle one (5 to 20 MB for
every installation); TROUBLESHOOTING says where to get one and where it
goes. Until then the log names the characters once. Diagnostics:
`map_fallback_fonts`.

## Unusual missions on Classic robots, once iRobot's numbers arrive

A Classic robot reports no dirt count. It comes with iRobot's mission
history minutes after the mission ended -- after
`roomba_plus_mission_completed`, which therefore never said `dirt_spike`
on a Classic robot, only the mission history did when asked. Now
`roomba_plus_mission_reassessed` says it: once per mission, when the
numbers for a mission of the last 24 hours make it unusual for another
reason than at its end. The run of unusual missions is checked again
then, too. The Exception notifications blueprint notifies on it
(*A mission iRobot's numbers show as unusual*, on by default). Needs the
iRobot login; a local-only robot never gets the numbers. Prime robots
had them at the mission end already.

**Removed: the "Possible floor accident detected" repair issue.** It was
listed since 3.5.0 but never raised: nothing called the check, and it
read field names the stored missions do not have. An old one is removed
at start. The `dirt_spike` anomaly above covers the case.

## Device triggers that never fired

*Cleaning started*, *Cleaning finished*, *Stuck*, *Docked* and *Error*
in the automation editor's device triggers **have not fired since
4.0.0**, on either generation. They compared the status sensors with
display words ("Running", "Charging", "None") while the sensors report
`running`, `charging` and, without an error, no value. They now use the
sensors' own values, from the one table both generations share, and a
test drives them through Home Assistant itself. Refinements while
there:

- *Cleaning started* no longer fires when the robot carries on after a
  mid-mission recharge or bin emptying, or when a docked robot only
  empties its bin.
- *Cleaning finished* also fires when the mission ends with the Clean
  Base emptying the bin.
- *Docked* no longer fires again whenever a docked robot tops up from
  100 % to 99 %.
- None of them fires when Home Assistant restarts with the robot
  already docked, stuck or in an error.

The status sensor reads *Emptying bin* while a docked robot empties its
bin on command; it said *Running*.

## Demand cleaning on Prime robots

A Prime robot rates how dirty each room is and sends the value from
which a room needs cleaning. Roomba+ had the code to act on that and
never ran it: the option in Configure aborted on Prime, nothing asked
the robot for its scores, and `auto_clean_dirty_rooms` chose from the
Classic dirt index, which never qualified a room on Prime, so it always
cleaned the whole house.

- **Configure → Demand cleaning** is offered on Prime, with the switch
  alone (the robot sends its own threshold, so there is no multiplier).
- **After each finished mission** Roomba+ asks the robot and starts the
  dirtiest room past its threshold:
  - one room, at most every 6 hours (as on Classic);
  - only with the robot on its dock, and only when presence scheduling
    and the blocking sensors allow a start -- asked again right before
    the command, so a run started meanwhile is not overridden;
  - on the floor the robot is on: the scores and the room come from the
    map it stands on, not from the map select, and with no known
    location nothing starts;
  - only after a mission Home Assistant watched run: not for missions
    that ran while it was down, not after the six-hourly history check.
- **`auto_clean_dirty_rooms`** on Prime cleans the rooms the robot rates
  dirty, dirtiest first (`max_rooms` caps them), on the floor the robot
  is on (else the map select); with none, the whole house as before.
- With demand cleaning on, the mission sensor's `unfinished_rooms`
  attribute fills whenever the scores are read: after a mission when
  the start conditions hold, and on every `auto_clean_dirty_rooms`.
- **Presence scheduling can be switched on for Prime robots.** The
  manager has worked on Prime since 4.0.0a31, but Configure only offered
  the step to robots reporting the Classic `schedHold`.
- **An active blocking sensor now stops demand cleaning, on both
  generations.** Only a start already queued by a blocking sensor did;
  with the behaviour *abort* nothing is ever queued, so a sensor that
  was on did not keep the robot in.
- **Switching demand cleaning or presence scheduling on or off now takes
  effect at once** (the entry reloads), on both generations. Both are
  set up at start, and a change waited for the next restart. Entries
  that already had one on are prepared at setup, so the first unrelated
  options change does not reload them.

## Automatic learns the turn of the path

On robots whose position Roomba+ asks for (i7+, j7+ and others that do
not report it), *Align the path with the rooms* on *Automatic* took the
turn from the map the robot saved. That was wrong on two of the three
robots whose owners checked: @nareso's j7+ read 180°, an i7+ −90°, where
0° was right on both.

- Every requested position is tried at 0°, 90°, 180° and 270° and
  counted per map: how many land in a room.
- After 300 positions (about five minutes of cleaning), an angle that
  puts at least half of them in a room, and half as many again as the
  angle in force, becomes Automatic's. The path already drawn turns
  with it during the run; the next run starts with it.
- A map turned in the iRobot app gets new angles and starts its counts
  afresh. A fixed angle is never changed, but counted all the same.
- Diagnostics: `path_turn_learning` (counts, no map id) and
  `requested_path_turn_source: learned`. The counts are kept per robot
  and removed with it.

## Error texts from the robot's own app

> **Breaking** for an automation that compares a Classic robot's error
> text. Compare the code instead: `error_code` on the vacuum, or the
> *Error – Last code* sensor.

Until now one table answered for both generations: the Prime app's text
where it had one, our own wording for the rest. A Classic robot read
mostly ours, and sometimes a Prime text for a number the two apps use
for different faults (2, 6, 47, 106 and 228).

**Each robot reads its own app.** A Classic robot reads the texts of
iRobot Home 7.18, through roombapy-prime 0.8.0; a Prime robot the Prime
app's, as before. The other app's text appears only where the robot's
own has none: Prime-only numbers on a Classic robot, and on a Prime
robot the codes its app does not list. Where the app words an error for
a model, so does the integration: the Roomba 69x, the Essential (Q0, Y0)
and a robot on a Clean Base. Where iRobot's text names the robot, it
names yours, by the name of its entry.

**On a Classic robot the error sensor's state changes for 44 codes**;
64 stay as they were. It is the app's English text, in every language
of the interface, so automations keep working when the language
changes. Some examples:

| Code | Until 4.4 | 4.5.0 |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Debris extractors stuck | Brushes need cleaning |
| 15 | Reboot required | Robot rebooted |
| 36 | Bin full | Bin and sensors need cleaning |
| 89 | Mission runtime too long | Timed out |
| 91-93 | Workspace path error | Workspace path error: Retrain *name* |
| 102 | Charging error | Battery not charging |
| 451 | Tank low | Clean Base: tank low |
| 502 | Charging contacts need cleaning | Charging issue |

On a Prime robot twelve codes change, among them 15, 21, 38, 88 and 89,
which now read the Classic app's text instead of ours. 85, 86 and the
Clean Base codes 450-509 have no text on a Prime robot any longer: they
are Classic numbers.

**Our own texts remain for the battery and charging errors only**:
101-111, 114, 115, 117 and 119-121. The Classic app shows one line for
all of them ("Charging error") or points to the battery; ours say which
fault it is and what to do, each checked against iRobot's help article
for the code and corrected where it was not right -- 107 reads "Battery
too cold", 108 "Battery not charging" (its temperature, or not an
original iRobot battery), 109 "Permanent battery fault", and 110 says to
replace the battery. Every other own text is gone.

**iRobot's article for the error.** iRobot's help catalogue for the
model, loaded with the parts catalogue and without signing in, links an
article for most errors. It is `help_url` on *Error – Last code*, on the
vacuum and on the Prime error sensor, and in the error-recurrence
event; the repair issue an error brings for a care task links it as
*Learn more*. Where the catalogue has nothing for a code, the attribute is
absent.

**More places link it** (b4): *Status – Error* while it shows the error;
the readiness sensor on a refusal to start, with the Classic app's words
for it (`app_label`, `description`, in your language) beside the state's
key; the maintenance list, event and blueprint for an entry an error
brought (`help_url`, `notification_help_url`). Error 17 ("Path blocked")
was checked as a pointer to the camera window and left out: eight of
the ten steps on iRobot's page are about the room.

**Whose text it is:** `text_source` on *Error – Last code* and
`error_text_source` on the Prime error sensor -- `classic_app`,
`prime_app` or `roomba_plus`. `classic_label_unconfirmed` on the Prime
error sensor is gone; the case it flagged no longer arises.

**The care task an error points to** (maintenance list, care guide,
repair issue) was checked code by code against iRobot's help pages:

- *Classic:* 1, 3 and 13 now point to the wheels, 121 to the charging
  contacts, 8 to the filter instead of the bin. 6 and 25 ("stuck near a
  cliff") show the cliff-sensor guide but do not make the cleaning due:
  usually it is a real edge or a dark floor. 11, 20, 27, 38, 68, 78, 79,
  102 and 103 no longer point to a care task; iRobot's pages give none.
- *Prime:* besides 2, 6, 201, 1000 and 1001, now 4 and 5 (wheels), 14
  (bin), 26 (filter), 104, 105, 119 and 121 (charging contacts) and 517
  (wash dock), from iRobot's Prime help pages.

**Clean Base status** has four more states the app knows: stopping
(304), motor problem (354), bag almost full (355) and hardware issue
(365).

**Readiness** names nine more values a Classic robot sends (14, 17, 19,
27, 40, 65, 73, 74 and 271), and 37 and 38 read as the app reads them --
battery failed, charge timeout -- instead of both as a battery
authentication error.

## Smaller fixes

- **A Prime robot's runtime of 56 years** (@jdmasuta, Roomba Plus 410
  Combo, `runtimeStats.hr` 497874 beside 476 powered-on hours): the
  *Lifetime runtime* sensor reads unknown for a runtime longer than the robot
  has been switched on, or than ten years where that is not known;
  `reported_hours` and `rejected` say what was reported and why. His
  counter counts correctly from an impossible start, so on such a robot
  the sensor hides itself while that holds; your own choice to hide or
  show it stands.
- **The rooms under the cleaning path, and the room map, empty after a
  mission** (@pk-1966, i7+). After a mission the iRobot cloud can make
  its own processing of the robot's upload the active map version
  (`creator: "cloud"`), and that version has no rooms. Roomba+ read the
  rooms from it; it reads them from your own version now, the one the
  app, the schedule and the room commands use. Versions the robot made
  itself were already passed over.
- **Prime map: the inner edge of a border area** is drawn too
  (@Rickeeh, PR #223); an area with a hole showed only its outer edge.
- **A Smart Map robot's map that did not load at startup** is asked
  for again every 10 minutes, up to six times, and the log says so at
  info level. Before, nothing asked again until the next mission ended
  or a day later.
- **SmartScrub per room on Prime** (@gunzxz, Plus 405 Combo):
  `smart_scrub` in `clean_room` goes out as `swScrub` per region instead
  of being dropped (4.5.0b6). Not yet confirmed on a robot; a robot
  that does not take the field ignores it.
- **"Roomba+ needs the asynchronous roombapy 2.x" when adding it**
  (@gdenas): the text said deleting the built-in Roomba entry ends it,
  but Home Assistant's discovery still loads the built-in integration
  after every restart. The text says so and links the troubleshooting
  guide, which points to the workaround.
- **A maintenance reset on a Prime robot was booked at 0 hours** (since
  4.0.0a14, a key no robot sends). It reads the *Lifetime runtime*
  meter now, with the same check.
- **The robot's area counter in a cloud summary** is scaled by 100, as
  the iRobot app does; it would have shown a hundredth of the area.
- **Clean in sequence** (`clean_sequence`) ended with neither robot's
  entry: after an unload or reload it could still start the second
  robot. Unloading either now ends it, and a delayed start waiting at
  that moment is cancelled.
- The statistics backfill after a Prime history sync is tied to the
  entry, so an unload waits for it. It only reads the mission store; an
  interrupted run cannot damage it. If it cannot be started, it is
  closed instead of ending as "coroutine ... was never awaited".
- **Diagnostics:** keys of a cloud login are redacted in camel case,
  snake case or with hyphens -- `AccessKeyId`, the Cognito identity, an
  IoT endpoint -- beside the password, secret and token shapes already
  caught. None is in the diagnostics today.
- **Privacy page:** the Classic cloud data is read once a day and after
  each mission (not every 15 minutes), and the mission archive comes
  from iRobot's mission history.
- **900-series room detection no longer holds up Home Assistant.** It ran
  on the event loop after each mission, about 0.1 s on a real 980 grid
  and about 1.5 s on a large home; it now runs in the background.
- **Less in the recorder.** Room outlines, calibration points, zones,
  doors and furniture on the map images, the per-room dictionaries of
  *Rooms overdue* and the nested copy under `rooms` on *Room cleaning
  history* are no longer written to the database with every update.
  Cards read the current state and are unaffected; the flat per-room
  keys and `overdue_rooms` stay recorded.
- **Charging and battery error texts** follow iRobot's help article for
  each code: 117 (battery not recognised) and 121 (docked but not
  charging) added; 102, 103, 107-111 and 120 said something else. 53,
  122 and 123 appear in no iRobot source and are removed, as 130-163
  were in 4.4.0: a robot reporting one shows the code. See [Error texts
  from the robot's own app](#error-texts-from-the-robots-own-app).
- **Background work ends with the integration.** Store saves, the
  presence and blocking timers, the parts refresh after a Prime mission
  and the rest no longer run on after an unload or reload: an unload
  waits for the short ones and cancels the long timers.
- The Prime live map downloads through roombapy-prime, with its timeout;
  our own download had none. A timeout or a dropped connection is
  counted and logged as a failed download, not as a payload that did
  not decode.
- After a 900-series mission the map shows the new room areas as soon
  as they are computed, not only at the next update.
- *Clean in sequence* no longer leaves an unload callback behind on both
  entries with every call.

## Maintenance: one status, one way to mark it done

Every part and care task is now one maintenance entry, worked out once
and read by every surface; the to-do list, the settings page and the
care guides below are built on it.

- **`sensor.<robot>_maintenance`** -- ok, soon, due or overdue, both
  generations. Soon from 80 % of the interval, due at 100 %, overdue a
  week later; a care task is due on its date and overdue after half an
  interval more. `items` lists every entry: what is left and in which
  unit, when it is due, the interval and where it comes from, when it
  was last done, and the guide and shop links from iRobot's parts
  catalogue.
- **`event.<robot>_maintenance`** -- `due`, `overdue`, `done`,
  `snoozed`, once per change, not once per restart. A reset in the
  iRobot app counts as done too.
- **`roomba_plus.maintenance_done`** and
  **`roomba_plus.maintenance_snooze`**. Done takes an entry id such as
  `replace.filter` or `clean.wheel`; a part to replace can be reported
  as only cleaned, and its counter stays. **On a Prime robot the reset
  goes to iRobot**, as the app sends it; if iRobot applies it to no
  part, the action says so instead of succeeding.
- **`GET /api/roomba_plus/{entry_id}/maintenance`** -- the entries for
  a card.
- The part sensors carry `level`, `used`, `due`, `last_replaced`,
  `wear_rate` and `buy_url`; Prime part sensors also `counter` and
  `reset_by`.
- **A Prime part is named the way the app names it**: by the first of
  ten words found in its name (mop washing system, dock washing system,
  main brush, side brush, detergent, filter, pad, bag, roller, sensor).
  Ids matched against the app come first. Detergent is a part now.
- **Care tasks on Prime** with the 2025 lines' intervals: side brush,
  cliff sensors and charging contacts every two weeks. A robot that
  counts its cliff sensors itself gets no calendar reminder beside it.
- Diagnostics: every part as reported, with its catalogue name and
  role, so unidentified Prime parts (202, 212, 68) can be settled from a
  download.

### The maintenance list, on both generations

- **Built from the same entries** and **open from "due"**: while
  nothing is due, nothing is open. "Soon" shows on the status sensor and
  the part sensors.
- **On for a robot added from 4.5.0 on.** A robot set up before keeps
  its setting.
- **Prime items can be ticked off** -- the reset goes to iRobot, the
  way the app sends it. Until now the Prime list was read-only. **Not
  yet confirmed on a Prime robot in the field**: if iRobot does not take
  the reset, Home Assistant says so rather than showing it as done.
- **The description is formatted text**: what is left, the error that
  brought the item, and links to iRobot's guide and the part.
- **An error that points to a care task brings a "check" item** while
  the robot reports it, and it goes with the error -- "Check the main
  brushes" for a brush error. A task with an item of its own carries
  the error there instead, so never two items for one task. With care
  reminders on, a dirt error (filter, brushes, side brush, cliff
  sensors) makes the cleaning due until it is ticked off. On Prime only
  the errors the app's pictures tie to a part: 2, 6, 201, 1000, 1001.
- Item ids stay as they were (`filter_maintenance`, `care_wheel`,
  `part_147`).

### Configure → Maintenance, a lean start, a maintenance calendar

- **One page for maintenance**: an overview of every entry (level,
  what is left, where the interval comes from), the settings, and
  "report a part replaced or a task done". The list, cleaning reminders
  and pets moved here from Settings. New: "soon" from 80 % / 60 % /
  never, where an error's care guide appears, a repair issue per part a
  week overdue (off), the maintenance
  calendar, hiding the diagnostic entities, and an hour interval per part
  on Classic (empty = automatic), side brush and Clean Base bag included.
- **A new installation is lean**: "last replaced", "wear rate", "days
  until due" and the battery, wheel, contact and bin buttons are created
  disabled. A 980 starts with nine maintenance entities. An existing
  installation keeps its entities; *Hide the maintenance diagnostic
  entities* takes it to the same set and back.
- **`calendar.<robot>_maintenance`**, read-only and off by default: the
  due dates as all-day events, a part's date marked as an estimate.
- **The collective "Maintenance is overdue" repair issue is gone**, and
  an open one is removed at start-up: it could not be fixed from the
  issue. The status, the list and the event say it where it can be
  acted on.

### Care guides

- **A guide for every care task and part**, written and drawn for
  Roomba+, in all eight languages: what the part does, how to clean or
  replace it in three or four steps, what not to do, and a drawing of
  the robot's underside, bin, Clean Base or wash dock with the part
  highlighted -- amber, with a halo and a numbered marker, in light and
  dark mode. The drawing matches the series: 600-900, e/i/j, s, Braava;
  a Prime robot draws the e/i/j underside. iRobot's guide for the model
  is linked below it where the parts catalogue has one.
- **Configure → Maintenance → Care guides**: the robot's guides, the
  most urgent first; the chosen one with its drawing, and "mark as done
  now" where it has an entry.
- **Linked from the maintenance list, the calendar and the event**
  (`guide`), and from the vacuum while an error points to a care task:

### From @msva17's report (#200)

- **A learned interval can be forgotten**: Configure → Maintenance →
  *Forget learned intervals*, or `roomba_plus.maintenance_forget_learned`,
  for one part or all. The way back from a reset pressed by mistake or a
  part swapped early, without editing the storage file. The current
  part's count stays; learning starts again with the next two
  replacements. Classic robots; a Prime robot's intervals are its own
  counters.
- **A half report from the cloud is no longer taken as the baseline.**
  Right after a reset, iRobot answered for a while with the old part's
  minutes used and no minutes remaining; taken as the new part's
  baseline, the filter read near zero and Maintenance due came on. The
  baseline now waits for the first report that carries both.
- A j7+ reports the same part ids as the j9+ (46, 47, 48, 139).

Fixed on the way:

- **The remaining hours ignored the series profile.** The sensor took
  the hour option or the 52-hour fallback while the due check used the
  profile, so a 980 counted down from one figure and was due at another.
  Both read one calculation now.
- **iRobot's counters were read once, at setup.** A part replaced in
  the app showed as worn until a restart, and the remaining hours stood
  still between restarts. They follow every cloud poll now, and count on
  with the hours run in between.
- **A reset button left an i3+ or j9+ due until the next restart**: the
  stored cloud counter still read zero. A replacement recorded in Home
  Assistant after the cloud's last reset now stands, and the next poll
  does not undo it.
- **"Pad replaced" was on every Classic robot**, a 980 included, and
  moved the main-brush counter. It is on a Braava only; the button goes
  from robots without a pad.
