## 900-series areas froze when the coverage map shrank

On the maintainer's own 980 almost every area was wrong: one area held
half the flat, and others lay where the robot no longer went.

The areas held 5817 cells; the coverage map had 3056. Cells nothing
visits again fade out of the coverage map, so it shrinks as well as
grows, but the areas were only recomputed when it grew by 30 cells past
its size at the last recompute. A coverage map that had once been larger
never got there again, and the areas stayed as first drawn, for weeks.

- **The areas recompute when the coverage map has grown or shrunk by 30
  cells**, or holds 30 cells no area has.
- **An area the coverage map no longer has is removed** at the next
  recompute, unless you named or hid it. Until now it was kept whole and
  drawn over the areas that cover the same floor now: a recompute on
  that 980 kept all eleven old areas and added four new ones on top.
- **A named room keeps its name and place** while its floor fades, as
  behind a door kept shut for two weeks. Only the part no other area
  covers is drawn.
- **Areas that are right are not touched.** Checked on @liblit's store
  and two of the maintainer's own: none of them recomputes.

The same 980's coverage map also held a mission from 11 September that
iRobot records as started away from the dock: it fits the other
missions only turned by 33°, and 16% of the coverage map came from it
alone. 4.3.1 already keeps such missions out; what they left fades out
over about a dozen missions.

## Turn the map

The map has no north. Its top is the way the robot faced as it left the
dock, so it depends on how the dock stands; the maintainer's came out a
quarter turn off the way he pictures the flat. **Configure → Settings →
Map rotation** turns every picture clockwise by 90, 180 or 270 degrees:
the cleaning path with the robot's heading, doors and overlays, the area
map, the coverage map, and the cloud coverage a Smart Map robot's map
shows when it has no path. Nothing stored moves, and a path saved before
is turned on restore. Prime maps keep the app's orientation. The rooms
map of a Smart Map robot is not turned yet; see below.

## Smart Map robots: the cleaning path the right way round

@frnchfrgg's i-series on lewis firmware, one of the robots that only
report their position when asked, drew its live path flipped left to
right, exactly as the 980s did before 4.3.1. The flip comes from the
same place, a swap of the robot's two axes in Roomba+, which every
Classic robot goes through. 4.3.1 mirrored the pictures back on the
900-series only, waiting for a Smart Map robot to be checked against its
floor; this is that check. The cleaning path and the coverage map are
now mirrored back on every Classic robot. Nothing stored changes.

Pictures from the iRobot cloud were never flipped: the rooms map and the
coverage of the last mission. They do not follow the turn set in the
iRobot app either, and never have: they show the map as it was right
after mapping. Mirrored back, the live path does follow the app's turn
on his robot. The angles the map carries are now in the diagnostics
(`pmap_versions` → `orientation_rad`), which is what drawing the rooms
map the app's way round needs.

**For card authors:** the coverage map's `render_extent_mm` also carries
`mirror_x` and `quarter_turns`. With `p` and `q` the pixel position the
4.3.1 rule gives, divided by `size_px`, each clockwise quarter turn takes
`(p, q)` to `(1 − q, p)`.

The list of names said nothing about which area was which. On a

## j-series parts

@msva17 read his j9+'s parts in the iRobot app against their attributes:
46 is the filter, 47 the side brush, 48 the main brushes, and the bag is
139 as on the i3+. The order of the i3's 35/36/37, shifted by eleven.
Until now these parts had no role: the maintenance sensors had no
remaining hours from iRobot, and a reset was not sent to iRobot. 47 is
the side brush by elimination; it and the bag had both just been reset.

## Maintenance by iRobot's own numbers

**Replacement intervals follow the robot's series.** Where neither
iRobot's counter for the robot, nor your own replacements, nor an hour
option give an interval, it used to be one round figure for every robot:
filter 60 h, main brushes 200 h. The per-series values the robot
profiles have carried since 2.x were never used. They are now, after
the field data that exists: the i-series takes an i3+'s cloud counters
(filter 52 h, main brushes 312 h, side brush 157 h; the research value
for the brushes was half that), the j-series' main brushes @msva17's
j9+ counter (312 h), and a Braava no longer carries a filter. A 600 with
an `R6…` SKU gets the 600's parts instead of the 900's. Models without
a profile (Combo, unknown) fall back to the i3+'s budgets, as does the
Clean Base bag (29 h). Each part sensor says where its interval comes
from in `interval_source`, and carries iRobot's month guidance in
`replace_every_months` where iRobot publishes one.

**Cleaning reminders, if you want them.** Off by default: **Configure →
Settings → Cleaning reminders** switches them on; off, there is no
reminder sensor, no "cleaned" button and no cleaning item. iRobot's care
articles give cleaning intervals in days for every series: filter and main brushes weekly, twice a week in
homes with pets; side brush monthly; wheels every two weeks; cliff
sensors and charging contacts monthly. The new sensor **Next cleaning
due** shows the earliest, with the full schedule as attributes, and
four new buttons record a cleaning: filter, main brushes, side brush,
cliff sensors (with the existing wheels and contacts buttons). A Braava
is reminded of its sensors and contacts only. **Configure → Settings →
Pets in the household** halves the filter and brush intervals. The
reminders count from the moment they are switched on, so nothing is
overdue on day one, not even after a wheels cleaning recorded years
ago.

**The Classic maintenance list said "clean" and did "replace".** Its
brush item read "Clean brush roll", and ticking it reset the main-brush
replacement counter, so somebody cleaning the brushes weekly as invited
never saw the replacement come due. The list is rebuilt:

- **A replacement item per part the robot has**, ticked off exactly like
  its reset button, including iRobot's counter, which the list never
  updated before.
- **A cleaning item per care task** with the reminders on, which moves
  no counter.
- **In Home Assistant's language.** The list was English everywhere.
- **Items not due are listed as done** with their due date, as on Prime,
  so the open count means something.

## A robot of unknown model is set up as Prime

Classic is the older generation and complete; every new model is
Prime. Setup still sent a robot whose SKU it did not recognise down the
Classic path: after the iRobot login it asked for the robot's local IP
address, and the local robot list offered it, where picking it did
nothing. Such a robot is now set up as Prime, from the account, and the
local list shows only robots known to be Classic. A Classic robot that
reports no SKU can still be added with "Add manually".

## A mission stored twice in the trajectories

The same 980's trajectory store held 2 October twice, identical to the
point. The end of that mission was processed a second time from a
mission checkpoint that had outlived it, resumed at the next start of
Home Assistant, and the coverage map counted the mission twice too.

- **A mission is stored once.** A store already holding one twice is
  cleaned when Home Assistant starts.
- **A checkpoint of a mission already recorded is discarded** instead
  of being processed again.
- **No checkpoint is written for a mission that has just ended.** The
  save at dock contact could copy the whole path a moment before the
  same message ended the mission.

How the checkpoint outlived its mission is not proven; the first two
hold either way.

## Collecting what two open questions need

Two questions have waited since September for one observation each.
Both are now recorded when they happen, under `field_evidence` in the
diagnostics, with no positions.

- **Prime readiness above 10.** Whether a Prime robot sends `notReady`
  as the wire value or as the app's index is not known, and the two
  differ: wire 15 is "Insufficient charge", index 15 "In cloud
  upgrade". The readiness sensor shows what it showed, and now also
  carries both readings as `as_index` and `as_wire`. Each change is kept
  with battery, phase and dock state, so one refusal with a known cause
  decides it. If your Prime robot refuses to start with a code above
  10, a diagnostics download settles this.
- **A room start into a paused mission** (Classic, @AlakazipLabs). The
  warning for a swallowed command cannot see this case: the mission is
  open before and after, and every recorded attempt was refused, so
  there is nothing to compare a success against. Such a start is now
  kept with the mission state and the robot's last command before and
  after.
